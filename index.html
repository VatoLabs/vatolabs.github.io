Where it stands

1. Check the servos

so101-doctor reads the settings of your six servos and warns about the ones that are off. It cannot move the arm or change a setting.

Released Install and run it. Free, Apache-2.0.

2. Simulate the real servo

A MuJoCo twin (a simulated copy) of the SO-101 with the servo’s real control loop. It rests about twice as close to the real arm as the stock model.

Built by hand first

Before software, hardware: the last robot here was designed, printed, wired and cast from scratch, and it jumps 41 cm.

Why this lab

I’m Vato. I studied mechanical engineering at Warwick and start an MEng at Berkeley in Fall 2027. Until then, I’m building this.

Cheap robot arms are good enough to do real work. What holds them back is that their servos are rarely modelled, so the arm doesn’t go where the policy tells it to.

The goal: a $200 arm makes my breakfast every morning, learned from a video of my own hands. That needs an arm that goes exactly where it is told, so that is where I’m starting.

Every result here comes with its trial count, its failures and its cost. If it breaks, you’ll see it break.
